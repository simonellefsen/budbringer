The Postilion/ Graphics review/ 16 Aug 2026/ three.js r170 · vanilla TS

The world is a stage set with the lights switched off

I read the source and ran the game to measure it. The look isn't failing because the budget is spent — it's failing because nothing in the scene responds to light, the ink lines that define the reference style are geometrically broken, and the triangle budget is 1–3% used. All three are fixable in a defined order.

What the running game measures

Captured from renderer.info and a scene traversal at the town spawn point, 1280×800.

Those two coloured numbers are the whole story. The scene is carrying 3,530 distinct shader materials to draw 85,120 triangles — roughly one material per twenty-four triangles. A browser on this machine will happily push two to three million triangles a frame, so the entire visual budget is being spent on object count rather than on detail. There is something like 30× headroom sitting unused, and it is exactly the headroom a proper art pass needs.

Diagnosis — six specific causes

Why it reads as flat cardboard

01

Every light in the scene is inert

ToonMaterial is a raw ShaderMaterial that computes its own three-band ramp from a hand-set direction uniform. It never samples a shadow map, never reads a light, and never applies fog. Three lights are created in Game.setupLighting(), a 2048² shadow map is rendered every frame, and not one pixel is affected by any of it. The world has no cast shadows, no contact darkening, no ambient occlusion — which is precisely the depth cue the reference frames lean on hardest.

Measured: 3,717 of 3,719 meshes are ShaderMaterial; light-aware meshes = 0. 521 meshes carry castShadow = true into a shadow map nothing reads. The uLightColor uniform is declared, assigned, and never referenced in the fragment shader.

02

The ink outlines are broken, not merely thin

Outlines come from inverted hulls: a duplicated mesh, back-faces, vertices pushed along their normals. On BoxGeometry and ExtrudeGeometry the vertices are split with per-face normals, so pushing outward separates the box into six disconnected floating plates instead of growing a shell. I confirmed this by forcing thickness to 0.5 and the colour to magenta in the live scene: the houses sprouted detached slabs above their roofs, and the character was swallowed whole.

Thickness is also in world units, so a line's screen width changes with distance and with the size of the object it wraps — the opposite of the constant-weight pen stroke the reference is built on.

Measured: only 227 of 3,719 meshes have outline hulls at all — roads, props, NPCs and terrain have none. Meanwhile OutlinePass.ts, a 205-line screen-space edge detector that is far closer to the right answer, is never imported anywhere.

03

The palette is a stock CSS framework

0x2ecc71, 0x3498db, 0xe74c3c, 0xf39c12, 0x9b59b6, 0x2c3e50 — that is the Flat UI palette, used unmodified, at full saturation, next to pure-black roofs and pure-black roads. The reference works the other way round: chalky desaturated creams and green-greys carry ninety percent of the frame, and saturation is rationed to a single orange and a single teal.

There is no palette module. Hex literals are inline at roughly ninety call sites across Planet.ts, NPCManager.ts and Character.ts, so the look cannot be re-graded without a hunt.

Left six: shipping now. Right six: the reference's actual working range.

04

The road network drowns the world

createRoads() lays eleven latitude rings, twelve longitude lines, eight diagonals, six biome connectors and three concentric rings per biome — each segment a separate box mesh with its own material, plus separate meshes for the dashes. The result is that asphalt is the dominant surface of the planet and white dashes speckle every shot. The reference has almost no visible road: it has alleys, kerbs, stairs, gutters and thresholds.

Measured: the decorations group holds 2,199 children, the overwhelming majority of them road and dash segments.

05

The horizon is ten metres away and nothing hides it

At radius 30 with an eye height near 1.7, the horizon sits about 10 units from the character and 14 from the camera. Houses are 3.7 units tall. So every shot shows the ground curving away after three house-lengths, with flat sky above it — the "green ball" read. The reference never shows you a horizon; it terminates every sightline with a four-to-eight-unit wall, a stairway, or a building edge.

Consequence: fog is configured at 25–70 on a world where nothing is ever further than ~14 units away, so the fog never engages at all. Sky meets ground on a hard unmodulated seam.

06

Placement is uniform random, so nothing composes

fillGlobalDecorations() scatters 60 houses, 70 trees and 80 props at uniformly random spherical coordinates. Vending machines land in fields; benches face nothing; there is no clustering, no frontage, no negative space, no sense that a person arranged any of it. Charm in this genre comes from composition — a bicycle against a wall, a crate by a door, a wire crossing a gap.

Also worth fixing: CameraController.avoidBuildingCollision() traverses the whole scene and computes a fresh Box3 for all 3,719 meshes every frame. That is the single hottest thing in the update loop.

The target, taken apart

What the reference look is actually made of

The screenshots you shared aren't one effect, they're six layers stacked. Naming them separately is what makes the makeover tractable — each is a discrete piece of work with a discrete payoff, and they're worth roughly the proportions below.

Ink

Broken

Constant-weight dark lines on silhouettes and interior creases, with a faint hand-drawn wobble. Alone this is about half the style. It must be screen-space, so a line is the same pixel width on a doorknob and a lighthouse.

Two-band fill

Partial

One lit tone, one shadow tone, a hard terminator, and — critically — the shadow is tinted cool, never a darker version of the same hue. Big shadow shapes do the modelling that geometry doesn't.

Chalky palette

Missing

Bone, cream, warm grey, sage. Saturation rationed to one orange and one teal, spent on the things that matter: a lifebuoy, a door, a nameplate.

Paper grain

Missing

A subtle noise and vignette composited over the whole frame. Cheap, and it's what stops the image reading as "clean 3D". The current 0.02 dither in the fragment shader is a hundredth of what's needed and applied per-object rather than per-frame.

Dense human clutter

Missing

Railings, downpipes, AC units, crates, wires, planters, signage, stairs. This is where the unused 30× triangle headroom goes, and it's the layer Blender is for.

Corridor framing

Missing

Every shot walled on two sides, foreground occluder, mid-ground subject, sky as a slot rather than a field. Moderate FOV, camera low. Never a visible horizon.

Tooling

What you have, and what's missing

Blender MCP is installed but not connected

Blender 5.2 LTS is here and the Blender MCP add-on v1.5 is in your add-ons folder listening on port 9876 — but no MCP server is registered with Claude Code, so I can't drive Blender from this session. uvx is already on your PATH, so it's one command plus a session restart: 

claude mcp add blender -- uvx blender-mcp

Then open Blender, enable Blender MCP in Preferences → Add-ons, and hit Connect in the View3D sidebar. Nothing in phases 1–4 needs it; everything in phase 5 does.

Install now — all MIT or similar, all free

PackageWhat it's for
postprocessing pmndrs' effect composer. Merges passes into one shader and gives you proper multi-render-target depth and normals — which is where the ink pass, the grain and the vignette all live. Meaningfully faster than three's own composer.
three-custom-shader-material Lets you write the toon ramp on top of MeshStandardMaterial, so shadows, fog, lights and texture maps keep working. This is the direct fix for finding 01. A zero-dependency alternative is three's built-in MeshToonMaterial with a gradient map — start there if you want fewer moving parts.
tweakpane Live sliders for palette, light angle, band thresholds, ink width, grain strength. You cannot art-direct a look by editing hex literals and reloading; this is the difference between a week and an afternoon.
three-mesh-bvh Fast spatial queries for camera collision and ground snapping, replacing the per-frame full-scene Box3 sweep.
troika-three-text Crisp SDF text on 3D surfaces. Shop signs, banners and vertical signage carry a surprising amount of the reference's character, and they're currently absent entirely.
simplex-noise Coherent noise to replace Math.random() scatter, so props cluster into settlements instead of dusting the sphere evenly.
stats-gl On-screen draw-call and frame-time readout, so detail gets added against a visible number rather than a hunch.
@gltf-transform/cli Command-line inspect, dedupe, weld and Draco/meshopt compression for GLBs coming out of Blender. Run it as a build step once real assets land.

Worth it later, not yet

  • Playwright — screenshot a fixed set of camera positions on every commit. During a long art pass, catching "did that shader change break the shrine?" automatically is genuinely valuable. Free.
  • Material Maker — free, GPL, Godot-based procedural texture authoring, if you end up wanting tileable painted asphalt and concrete. Krita covers hand-painted work fine until then.
  • LibreSprite — free Aseprite fork, only if the UI goes pixel-art. Your reference's UI is chunky-pixel, so this is plausible.

Skip

  • React Three Fiber / drei — the project is vanilla TypeScript and healthier for it. The rewrite cost buys nothing the plan below needs.
  • Extra Blender add-ons — 5.2's stock glTF exporter is fine, and Grease Pencil (built in) covers hand-drawn texture work.
  • Inkscape — Affinity Designer already does this job for you.
Roadmap — the order is load-bearing

Six phases, each unblocking the next

The sequencing matters more than usual here. The ink pass needs clean depth and normals, which needs phase 1. Palette can only be judged against the ink, so it follows phase 2. And there's no point modelling a building kit before the street layout tells you what buildings are needed.

Ph 1

Put the lights back on

  • Replace ToonMaterial with MeshToonMaterial + gradient map, or CSM over MeshStandardMaterial.
  • Introduce a material cache — one material per palette entry, not one per mesh.
  • Delete all 227 inverted-hull outline meshes and the unused OutlinePass.ts.
  • Instance the repeated props: trees, poles, cones, mailboxes, road segments.

Buys: real cast shadows and contact darkening. 3,530 materials → a few dozen. The scene stops looking like unlit cardboard before a single asset is remade.

Ph 2

The ink pass

  • Screen-space edge detection over depth + view-normal buffers, composited dark.
  • Constant pixel width; small animated UV wobble for the hand-drawn quality.
  • Separate thresholds for silhouette edges and interior creases.

Buys: roughly half the target look, at a fixed per-frame cost that doesn't grow as you add detail. Highest visual return of anything on this list.

Ph 3

Grade and atmosphere

  • One palette.ts; every hex literal in the codebase moves into it.
  • Two-band ramp with a cool-tinted shadow and a warm rim.
  • Gradient sky dome and soft cloud cards, replacing the flat background colour.
  • Paper grain + vignette in the composite; refit fog to roughly 8–30.

Buys: the "painted, not rendered" read. Also makes every later decision cheap, because the whole grade is now four values in one file.

Ph 4

Rebuild the layout

  • Tear out the global road grid; author a handful of real streets and alleys instead.
  • Raise planet radius to roughly 60–80 and cut prop density to match.
  • Build walled corridors — buildings 4–8 units tall — so structures, never the horizon, close every sightline.
  • Replace random scatter with noise-driven clustering and hand-placed set pieces.

Buys: shots that frame like the reference. This is also where "walk around the whole planet" stops fighting "the street feels like a place".

Ph 5

Real assets, out of Blender

  • A kit: 10–14 building variants plus stairs, railings, awnings, downpipes, wires, signage, planters, crates.
  • Export GLB, instance everything, run gltf-transform as a build step.
  • Hand-painted texture work in Krita; signage typography in Affinity.

Buys: the clutter density that separates the reference from every other cel-shaded browser game. This is where the unused triangle headroom finally gets spent.

Ph 6

Character and interface

  • Model and rig the courier properly; export GLB with run, idle, hop and talk clips.
  • Rework the HUD to match — chunky nameplates, ink borders, the reference's UI voice.

Buys: the last thing the player looks at for the entire game stops being fourteen primitives with a sine-wave gait.

If you only do one thing

Start with phase 1

It's the least glamorous item on the list and it unblocks everything else. Right now a 2048² shadow map is rendered sixty times a second into the void, and no surface in the game knows where the sun is. Fixing that costs one material swap and a cache, and the difference will be visible in the first screenshot — before any new art exists at all.
